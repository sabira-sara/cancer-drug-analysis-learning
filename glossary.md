# Computational Oncology Glossary

## 1. Gene Expression

**Definition:** Gene expression is the process by which information encoded in DNA is used to produce functional RNA or proteins.

**Importance:** It helps researchers understand which genes are active or inactive in cancer cells.

**Example:** Increased expression of a gene associated with cell growth may contribute to tumour development.

---

## 2. Gene Regulation

**Definition:** Gene regulation refers to the mechanisms that control when, where, and how much a gene is expressed.

**Importance:** It helps researchers understand how abnormal gene activity contributes to cancer development.

**Example:** Changes in gene regulation may cause cancer cells to produce excessive amounts of proteins that promote cell growth.

---

## 3. RNA Sequencing (RNA-seq)

**Definition:** RNA sequencing is a molecular technique used to identify and measure the abundance of RNA molecules in a biological sample.

**Importance:** It helps researchers study gene expression patterns and identify genes that behave differently in cancer cells.

**Example:** RNA-seq can be used to compare gene expression between healthy tissue and tumour tissue.

---

## 4. Differential Gene Expression

**Definition:** Differential gene expression refers to differences in the expression levels of genes between two or more biological conditions.

**Importance:** It helps identify genes that may be involved in cancer development, progression, or treatment response.

**Example:** Researchers may identify genes that are significantly upregulated or downregulated in tumour samples compared with normal samples.

---

## 5. Gene Dependency

**Definition:** Gene dependency describes how much a cell relies on a particular gene for its survival, growth, or proliferation.

**Importance:** Identifying gene dependencies helps researchers discover potential therapeutic targets in cancer cells.

**Example:** If a cancer cell cannot survive when a particular gene is knocked out using CRISPR-Cas9, that gene may represent a dependency.

---

## 6. CRISPR-Cas9

**Definition:** CRISPR-Cas9 is a genome-editing technology that allows researchers to modify specific DNA sequences.

**Importance:** It helps researchers investigate gene functions and identify genes essential for cancer cell survival.

**Example:** Researchers can use CRISPR-Cas9 to knock out a gene and observe how cancer cell growth changes.

---

## 7. Ferroptosis

**Definition:** Ferroptosis is an iron-dependent form of regulated cell death characterized by the accumulation of lipid peroxides in cellular membranes.

**Importance:** Studying ferroptosis may help researchers understand cancer cell survival and develop new therapeutic strategies.

**Example:** Researchers may investigate whether activating ferroptosis can kill cancer cells that are resistant to conventional treatments.

---

## 8. Apoptosis

**Definition:** Apoptosis is a form of programmed cell death that occurs through a regulated cellular process.

**Importance:** Understanding apoptosis helps researchers investigate how cancer cells avoid normal cell death and continue proliferating.

**Example:** Some cancer cells develop mechanisms that suppress apoptosis, allowing them to survive longer than normal cells.

---

## 9. Cancer Cell Line

**Definition:** A cancer cell line is a population of cancer-derived cells that can be maintained and grown under laboratory conditions.

**Importance:** Cancer cell lines provide experimental models for studying cancer biology, gene function, and drug responses.

**Example:** HeLa cells are a widely used human cancer cell line originally derived from cervical cancer.

---

## 10. Biomarker

**Definition:** A biomarker is a measurable biological characteristic that indicates a normal biological process, disease condition, or response to treatment.

**Importance:** Biomarkers can help researchers detect diseases, predict treatment responses, and monitor disease progression.

**Example:** HER2 overexpression is a biomarker used to identify certain breast cancers and guide targeted treatment.
